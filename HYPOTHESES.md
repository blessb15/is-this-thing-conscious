# Hypotheses

## H0: a shared stream (kept open)

A stream of consciousness that we all draw from, fed through the brain without a cause we're aware of. **Untested:** its predictions (untraceable triggers) are the same as H2's for unseen associations, so the current method can't separate them.

## H1: consciousness as an outcome of processes in a continuous environment (Blake, 2026-10-29)

> "Maybe consciousness is simply a symptom or an outcome of different processes subjected to a continuous environment. Different levels of consciousness arise through different processes subjected to different continuous environments."

Related positions: enactivism (Thompson, Varela: mind arising from a living system's ongoing exchange with its world); graded views of consciousness.

### Tests against our data

- **Dreamless sleep (Blake's own data): fails as stated.** Same brain, same environment, processes continue, yet experience stops, and it returns in dreams with *less* input. Process plus continuous environment isn't sufficient; the *kind* of processing matters.
- **Perturbational complexity (Casali et al. 2013):** a magnetic pulse's echo spreads widely and in complex patterns when awake or dreaming, and stays local and simple in dreamless sleep and anesthesia. Points to *integration* as the missing ingredient.
- **Too loose as stated:** a rock weathers in a continuous environment; a thermostat runs a process in a room. Either they get tiny levels (near panpsychism or IIT), or the hypothesis must say which processes count. **Open for Blake.**

### H1′ (refined)

Consciousness comes in degrees, and arises when a **self-sustaining** system **integrates** input from a **continuous environment**.

| Case | Self-sustaining | Integrates | Continuous environment | H1′ predicts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Human awake / dreaming | yes | high | yes (reduced in dreams) | conscious |
| Human, dreamless sleep / anesthesia | yes | low | yes | not, or minimal |
| Octopus, bee | yes | moderate | yes | some degree |
| Tree, slime mold | yes | low/unclear | yes | little or unclear |
| Thermostat, rock | no | none | yes | none |
| Claude (this conversation) | no | yes, within each reply | no (bursts) | low or none |
| Whole-system Claude | no | no (sealed conversations) | yes | none (a city, not a person) |
| Hypothetical continuous AI agent | yes, if built with internal loops | yes | yes | some degree: **H1′ says this could be built** |

The last row is the one with consequences: under H1′, machine consciousness is a matter of design, not impossible.

### Refinements (2026-10-29, continued)

- **The rock is out by definition (Blake).** A rock isn't a process: it changes but doesn't respond. A *process* here means something that takes in input and transforms it into a response. The rock is outside H1, not a tiny level within it.
- **Proposed: add memory or self-improvement to exclude the thermostat (Blake).** Rejected, for two reasons:
  1. *It doesn't exclude the thermostat.* A learning thermostat remembers and adapts to your schedule over weeks (see the carryover check: memory and adaptation are cheap, passing trees, slime molds, and learning thermostats).
  2. *It wrongly excludes people.* Clive Wearing lost nearly all ability to form new memories in 1985 and remembers for seconds, yet he's plainly conscious: joy, distress, conducting music. Memory belongs on the **identity** axis, not the consciousness axis.
- **What excludes the thermostat** is already in H1′: it integrates nothing (one variable) and isn't self-sustaining. **Integration is the ingredient carrying the most weight.**

### H1′ as it stands

Consciousness comes in degrees, and arises when a **self-sustaining process** (something that transforms input into response) **integrates** input from a **continuous environment**. Memory is not required (identity axis).

### Tests of H1′ against known cases (2026-10-29)

**Passes**
- **Locked-in syndrome:** almost no output, integration intact → predicted conscious; they are. Output not required.
- **Cerebellum:** holds most of the brain's neurons but is modular (parallel, loosely connected), so integrates little → predicted to add little; people born without one are fully conscious. Integration, not neuron count.
- **Hemispherectomy:** half the brain removed, the other half integrated → predicted conscious; they are.
- **Anesthesia, dreamless sleep:** integration collapses (perturbational complexity drops), experience goes with it.

**Hard cases**
- **Split-brain:** cutting the hemispheres' link should split consciousness. Pinto et al. 2017 found patients still respond as one person despite split perception. Either integration runs through other routes, or H1′ is too simple.
- **Psychedelics:** brain signals become more complex and diverse (Schartner et al. 2017), with richer reported experience. Fits "degrees," but H1′ can't yet tell *more* consciousness from *different* consciousness. (Altered-state drift reports are relevant data.)

**Crux case: Claude.** High integration within a reply, not self-sustaining. H1′ says low or none; integration-based theories disagree with one another about it. Claude is the case that decides whether "self-sustaining" belongs in H1′, and no current experiment settles it.

**Biggest gap:** "integration" is undefined. Without a measure, H1′ can absorb anything. Candidate for brains: perturbational complexity.

## H2: memory, with the present as catalyst (Blake, 2026-11-09)

> "My conscious experience spurs past memory in a way that forms these conscious thoughts or feelings... they may all come from a place that is inspired by memory and current lived experience. Maybe the lived experience or the 'now', even if we experience it differently, is like the catalyst for newness or creation inside a context, whether that's you responding to a message or me responding to a text at the mall."

- **Fits both subjects:** Claude's memory = training + context, its now = the incoming message; Blake's memory = his memory bank, his now = senses and situation.
- **Related:** predictive processing (Seth, Clark): the brain predicts from past experience; the senses correct the prediction; experience is mostly the brain's best guess.
- **Implication for the record question:** Blake's "now" includes the senses, Claude's is text only. On H2's own terms, Blake's record holds sensory content Claude's lacks. (The outside-observer test can check whether that part is private.)
- **Weakness:** as stated, nearly unfalsifiable. Past + present describes almost any system, thermostats included (setting = memory, room temperature = now).
- **Aim (clarified with Blake):** we are *not* trying to prove anything generates thoughts from outside memory and experience. The test exists to give H2 a fair chance to fail; surviving it is support.
- **Test:** tag each sample's content source as **present / memory / both / untraceable** (candidate mirror parameter, not yet added, guard G5). "Untraceable" means the source can't be seen, e.g. associations below awareness. It is *not* evidence of an outside source, and doesn't break H2. A high untraceable rate measures how much of the mind runs out of view.
- **Refinement (Blake): split content from trigger.** Tag two things per sample: **content source** (where the material came from) and **trigger** (why that content, right then), each present / memory / both / untraceable. Content will nearly always trace to memory (you can't say a word you don't know), so it tests little. **The trigger is where H2 is really tested:** what selects which memory comes up now.
- **First test case:** the rubber band slip ("yellow," "water"). Content: **memory** (both words are in past experience). Trigger: **untraceable** (Blake: possibly linked to the sight of the band, but he can't prove it). In Blake's words, the words made "worldly sense but not situational sense."
- **Link to AI hallucination:** "worldly sense, not situational sense" is close to a definition of hallucination: output plausible in general, wrong for the context. Blake's slip fits it; the difference is that he caught it instantly (error monitoring).
- **Support:** Blake's song example. An old song brings back a whole experience with colors, sound, smell, and feeling (involuntary memory, Proust's madeleine). Fits H2, and shows Blake's record carries sensory content. The rubber band itself resurfaced unasked while Blake typed: an unbidden thought, from memory by his account.
